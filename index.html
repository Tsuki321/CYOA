The Mages Association Bureau

A Magical Awakening Awaits

Reality always carried a layer we had no instruments to read. A corporation that no longer exists learned to read it, then handed the key to a government that has never let go. What follows is the shape of the world you are about to enter.

Atlas Corporation—a privately held research conglomerate headquartered at the Meridian Campus outside Boulder, Colorado—spent most of its existence in applied materials science and high-energy physics contracting. In 2014, an instrumentation array running a routine vacuum-fluctuation experiment logged a persistent energy gradient that matched no known field. Over the following fourteen months Atlas's physics division isolated, contained, and partially characterized the source: a higher-dimensional energy that saturated ordinary space yet interacted with standard matter only under narrow, reproducible conditions. Internal memos gave it a placeholder designation that stuck—Mana. The team that confirmed it stopped going home; division badge logs recorded a 300% jump in after-hours access over the next quarter.

Atlas had neither the capital nor the security apparatus to develop a discovery of that magnitude alone, and in 2015 it brought the data to the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency. DARPA's review board spent four months verifying the results across two independent laboratories before approving a joint black program: Project Zero Dawn, chartered with an initial $52 billion appropriation routed through unrelated budget lines and run out of a hardened complex in the Nevada desert redesignated Facility 09. Atlas supplied the science; DARPA supplied the oversight, the clearances, and the walls.

The program ran a little over two years, and in that span the foundations of an entire field were laid. Researchers mapped the dormant human organ that reacts to Mana—the Mana Reactor; built the first external processors able to shape raw Mana into controlled effects—the Intelligent Devices; formalized the mathematical programs those devices run—Spell Routines; and raised the first higher-dimensional protective fields that would become Barrier Jackets. The earliest mana-tech was crude, power-hungry, and dangerous to its operators, but it worked. By the time Zero Dawn wound down, the open question was no longer whether Mana could be harnessed, but who would own what came next.

Zero Dawn's chemists never manufactured Mana; they learned to detect, concentrate, and route an energy that was already everywhere. Mana is a higher-dimensional energy that permeates all of reality, occupying spatial relationships ordinary matter cannot reach while interacting with standard-model particles under specific conditions. Its defining and most unsettling property is responsiveness: inside a saturated field, Mana reacts in measurable ways to the focused intent of a conscious observer—a result that forced the program's physicists to treat cognition as a boundary condition rather than a nuisance variable.

Human exposure to concentrated Mana activates an organ that anatomy had never catalogued, because it lies dormant and sits only partially within baseline space. Designated the Mana Reactor, it occupies the same coordinates as the heart but extends into higher dimensions, functioning as a biological bridge that lets higher-dimensional energy do work in ordinary reality. Every human carries one from birth; fewer than one activation in several hundred thousand happens by accident, and the rest require deliberate, supervised induction. Once active, the reactor absorbs ambient Mana, stores it, and converts dietary and environmental intake at a rate fixed to the individual.

Reactor capacity varies across the population by nearly two orders of magnitude, and the program borrowed the Kabbalistic Tree of Life to classify it—ten ranks from Malkuth at baseline to Keter at the observed ceiling. Rank sets storage capacity, ambient absorption rate, and conversion efficiency. Genetic screening paired with dimensional-resonance imaging identifies an individual's rank with 94% accuracy before activation. A Keter-rank reactor holds and moves roughly 70 times the Mana of a baseline Malkuth, with absorption and recovery scaled to match.

Rank is immutable. It is set by developmental factors during gestation that no laboratory has learned to reproduce or alter, and no verified method raises it after birth. Training sharpens control, discharge efficiency, and routine complexity—never capacity. The ceiling is not merely theoretical: in more than a decade of universal screening, exactly one living person has ever imaged at Keter.

The reactor solved storage and supply; it did nothing for control. Shaping raw Mana into a specific effect in ordinary space demands dimensional mathematics that exceed unaided human cognition by three to four orders of magnitude, and Zero Dawn's early volunteers who attempted manual manipulation paid for it with seizures, cerebral hemorrhage, and lasting cognitive damage. The human nervous system is simply not rated for the computation.

The answer was to move the computation off the operator. An Intelligent Device is a palm-sized magitech construct that serves as the external processor for every mana operation its user performs. Each houses a purpose-built artificial general intelligence that handles the dimensional calculation and translates intent into effect. A bonding protocol keys the device to the user's genetic signature and the unique energy pattern of their reactor, forming a neural link that answers conscious command with sub-100-millisecond latency. A bonded device is also, in practice, a partner: its AGI learns its operator, offers counsel, and refuses commands that would injure them.

Devices execute Spell Routines—the mathematical programs that turn stored Mana into fire, force, barriers, or diagnosis. Routines are graded on a ten-tier scale by range, energy throughput, and computational load (operators call the last "weave"). Tier 1 routines work at room scale on modest power: illumination, ignition, simple kinetic shaping. The scale climbs through building, block, and district ranges to Tier 10—strategic effects whose outputs and processing demands press the limits of the best hardware ever built. The higher tiers are gated as much by a device's processing ceiling as by the operator's reactor.

Device technology has advanced through five generations, and today almost none of it is built by the government. First Generation devices are Civilian-grade: dependable for everyday routines, unsuited to combat, and as of 2026 still the most common hardware in private hands. Second and Third Generation devices are professional equipment—hardened materials, faster cores, AGI capable of limited autonomy. Fourth Generation is the current combat standard: quantum-enhanced processing, direct neural interface, tactical planning in contested conditions, priced for elite units and corporate security. Fifth Generation remains experimental, numbering in the dozens worldwide, with self-revising AGI, programmable metamaterials, and processing that approaches the limits dimensional physics imposes. Because the foundational principles were released openly years ago, every generation above the first is now designed and fabricated by competing private firms—which is why no two makers' devices feel quite alike.

Project Zero Dawn ended as a government program but not as an institution. When the charter lapsed, the Atlas research divisions that had carried the science refused to be folded back into a corporation that no longer grasped what it held. In 2017 they broke away and incorporated independently as The Numerals—a US-based research organization that kept its clearances, its people, and its data, and promptly cultivated the tightest possible relationship with the Department of Defense. Within two years the Numerals were less a contractor than a standing research arm of the US military, and the acknowledged premier authority on everything mana-tech.

The Numerals' most consequential decision was also its least guarded. In its early years the organization published a foundational set of mana-tech principles into the open—reactor induction theory, device-bonding architecture, the base Spell Routine instruction set, Barrier Jacket field geometry. The release was deliberate: the field was too large for any single body to build out, and open principles meant a thousand laboratories would do the engineering the Numerals could not. The bet paid off beyond projection. An industry grew in a handful of years where a monopoly would have taken decades.

What the open principles do not describe, the Numerals keep—fifth-generation AGI design, the hardest materials science, and the classified upper tiers of routine theory—and the rest they license. They sit beside the High Thaumaturgic Council and the Bureau as the third pillar of the American mana order: not a governing body, but the one everyone else calls when the physics stops making sense.

The open release that made the Numerals indispensable finished Atlas Corporation. The parent company had spun its science out and kept its name, its debt, and little else; it tried to compete in a market its own former divisions had just handed to the world for free. Atlas was eclipsed by its own offspring within five years, delisted, and filed for bankruptcy before the decade closed. The firm that discovered Mana now survives mainly as a footnote in Bureau orientation material and a logo on a handful of collectible first-generation shells.

What rose in its place was an industry. Hundreds of firms—from garage fabricators to defense primes—built real businesses on the Numerals' published principles, and competition did what competition does: it diversified everything. Rival houses developed proprietary device materials, from mass-market alloys to exotic engineered substrates; distinctive bonded forms; and signature AGI personalities, so that an experienced mage can often name a device's maker on sight. The variety of generations, materials, and shapes a mage chooses from is the fingerprint of a crowded, aggressive market, not of any central design.

The boom cut both ways. Over the following decade a minority of firms crossed the line their licenses drew—fabricating devices without Bureau tracking, selling combat-grade routines to private buyers, and running unlicensed reactor activations for anyone who could pay. The press calls them rogue corporations; the Bureau calls them the single largest source of uncontained mana-tech inside US borders. A device built to be untraceable, in the hands of an operator no agency screened, is the exact threat the regulatory apparatus was eventually built to answer.

When the Zero Dawn charter lapsed, the government faced a problem no statute anticipated: it had helped create individuals whom no court, prison, or conventional unit could reliably compel. A Keter-rank operator running fifth-generation hardware is not a citizen the ordinary machinery of the state can simply arrest. The answer, written into law in 2019, was to seat authority with those who could enforce it. The High Thaumaturgic Council is the apex mage authority of the United States—ten Seats, mapped by tradition to the ten Sephirot of the reactor scale, each held by one of the most capable mages the country has.

The Council answers to the President, and holds as its founding oath a duty to safeguard the state and obey its sitting Commander in Chief. Written into that same charter is a single reserved exception: should mana-tech be bent to abusive ends, or the state itself begin to go rogue on the strength of the power the Council guards, the First Seat holds the standing authority to refuse the order and ignore it. The Council invokes it sparingly—in practice it complies when given sound reason and justification, and treats that restraint as the point. For all the power it commands, the High Thaumaturgic Council has kept its oath: it answers to the office of the President, and turns against that office only when the office turns against the country. Within the body the First Seat's rulings stand unless the other nine overturn them by unanimous vote; Seats are earned through demonstrated capacity rather than political appointment, and each runs a domain of the national mana apparatus in addition to voting on policy.

The current composition, by Seat:

First Seat — Alicia Hindelburg Keter

Higher-dimensional theory, final policy authority · Fifth Generation grimoire-type, codename KEYSTONE

Half-German and half-American, Hindelburg is the first and only person ever to image at Keter rank, and by every metric the Bureau tracks the most powerful mage in the United States. She came up through the Numerals' theory division rather than the military, and her authority rests less on battlefield record—she has almost none—than on a capacity gap no rival can close. Her published work underlies the upper tiers of routine theory; her classified work is assumed to go further. Colleagues describe her as deliberate to the point of coldness, a trait the charter arguably demands of whoever casts a vote nine others must be unanimous to overrule.

Second Seat — Daniel Okafor Chokhmah

Arcaea Division command, overseas suppression · Fifth Generation saber-type, codename SENTINEL

Nigerian-American and former Army Special Forces, Okafor commands the Arcaea Division and its worldwide Suppression Teams. His remit is the hardest secret the country keeps—that mana-tech exists at all—and he is judged by the absence of evidence rather than its presence. A Chokhmah reactor and a close-quarters device make him a field commander who still deploys, which his staff count as both his greatest asset and a standing liability. Inside the High Thaumaturgic Council he argues for restraint abroad and overwhelming response only once containment has already failed.

Third Seat — Vivian Cross Chokhmah

Aerospace Fleet command, orbital and deep-space operations · Fifth Generation command-baton-type, codename FLAGSTAFF

American, a former naval aviator who transferred in during the first fleet trials, Cross commands the Aerospace Fleet from the flag deck of a Leviathan-class battleship she rarely leaves. She oversees solar-system patrol, the long-duration research flights, and the colony program, and she signed the orders that sent Voyager 4 past the Oort Cloud. Cross treats the Fleet as the strategic depth behind every other Seat's authority and resists any tasking that would pull warships back to low orbit for domestic display.

Fourth Seat — Soo-jin Park Binah

Numerals liaison, research policy · Fifth Generation analytic-grimoire-type, codename LEDGER

Korean-American, Park spent fifteen years inside the Numerals' routine-theory group before taking the Seat that speaks for it. She is the High Thaumaturgic Council's bridge to the organization that still understands the physics best, and the Seat most often outvoted—her instinct is to publish and standardize where her colleagues prefer to classify. Her Binah reactor and research pedigree make her the body's reference for what is and is not actually possible, a role she guards against colleagues inclined to promise more than the math allows.

Fifth Seat — Marcus Delgado Chesed

Bureau enforcement, registration and containment · Fourth Generation sidearm-type, codename WARRANT

Mexican-American and a career federal agent who awakened in the line of duty, Delgado runs the enforcement arm of the Mages Association Bureau and represents it at the table. He holds a Chesed reactor, modest beside most of his peers, and treats that as the point: the law has to apply the same way to a Malkuth shoplifter and a Geburah fugitive, or it applies to no one. His position that registration is a civil-liberties floor rather than a surveillance ceiling keeps him at odds with the Division seats.

Sixth Seat — Rajesh Varma Binah

Device engineering, generation standards · Fifth Generation fabrication-gauntlet-type, codename TOOLMARK

Indian-American, Varma trained as a semiconductor engineer before the field existed and now sets the standards every licensed manufacturer builds to. He can read a device's generation, maker, and condition from its field signature alone, and he personally certifies the few fifth-generation units that leave the labs. Inside the High Thaumaturgic Council he is the voice warning that open principles let capability outrun oversight, and he pushes for the tracking and shutdown features the Corporations would rather not fund.

Seventh Seat — Natalia Brandt Geburah

Combat doctrine, combined-arms integration · Fourth Generation command-standard-type, codename CHESSBOARD

German-American, Brandt wrote the doctrine that turned mage combat from individual dueling into coordinated operations folded into conventional force structure. A Geburah reactor and a career in operational planning give her the standing to tell generals how their new assets actually fight. She is blunt that mages are a supporting arm rather than a replacement for an army, and she treats the glamour the public attaches to combat mages as a tactical hazard in its own right.

Eighth Seat — Yusuf Haddad Binah

Biomantic medicine, reactor physiology · Fifth Generation scalpel-scepter-type, codename SUTURE

Lebanese-American and a trauma surgeon before his activation, Haddad built the biomantic medicine that lets mana-tech repair injuries conventional surgery cannot reach. His work on reactor physiology produced the supervised activation protocols now used nationwide, cutting accidental-awakening fatalities sharply. His Binah reactor and clinical caution make him the Seat most resistant to pushing any technique past its demonstrated limits; he will not sign off on cellular work whose long-term effects remain unmeasured.

Ninth Seat — Grace Nakamura Chesed

Threat assessment, intelligence synthesis · Fourth Generation sensor-array-type, codename CASSANDRA

Japanese-American and a former intelligence analyst, Nakamura runs the High Thaumaturgic Council's threat-assessment apparatus. Her routines do not read the future; they fuse dimensional-resonance surveillance with conventional intelligence into probability estimates precise enough to pre-position Suppression Teams and Bureau units before a situation breaks. A Chesed reactor keeps her off the front line, which she considers fitting for the person every major decision routes through for a risk read.

Tenth Seat — Owen Mackey Geburah

Counter-rogue-corporation operations · Fourth Generation intrusion-type, codename KILLSWITCH

Irish-American, Mackey came out of the Bureau's technical branch and now leads operations against rogue corporations—the firms fabricating untracked devices and running black-market awakenings. His intrusion-pattern device is built to breach hostile AGI and trigger the remote-shutdown pathway legitimate manufacturers are required to embed. A Geburah reactor lets him take the field against hardened targets personally, and he argues continually for the budget and legal reach to match a threat he insists is growing faster than the Bureau will admit.

The High Thaumaturgic Council is the United States' attempt to govern power that predates any law written to hold it. Ten people decide how mana-tech is researched, licensed, deployed, and contained; their votes reach from a civilian's first-generation device to the guns of the Aerospace Fleet. Every registered mage lives inside the structure they maintain—and the deliberate fault line running through it, the single Seat nine others must stand together to overrule, is the price the country accepted for seating authority where the power actually sits.

A bonded device is not locked to one shape. Within limits set by mass-energy conservation and its own generation, an Intelligent Device reconfigures into whatever form its routines call for—a blade, a cannon, a grimoire, a bare focus. That flexibility, more than any difference in reactor rank, is what sorted mages into three recognized Classes, each a coherent pairing of device configuration and fighting method rather than a rigid caste.

Knights fight close. Their devices take bladed or gauntleted forms, and their routines pour Mana into the body itself—speed, strength, durability—and into the edge they carry. A trained Knight closes distance faster than an unenhanced opponent can react, strikes hard enough to breach reinforced structures, and absorbs punishment that would kill a baseline human, trading the reach that defines most mage combat for the ability to end an engagement inside it. Their discipline is explosive, surgical, and favored wherever collateral damage must stay contained.

Bombardment mages fight from distance. Their devices become staves, cannons, and focusing arrays built to throw destructive routines across kilometers with real-time correction—the artillery of the field. The best can collapse a city block with a single routine or sustain a barrage that overwhelms a defender's barriers by volume, at the cost of the Knight's mobility and resilience. They dominate siege, area denial, and the elimination of high-value targets from beyond effective return fire, and they are most dangerous when someone else is keeping the enemy off them.

Thaumaturges fight by multiplying everyone else. Their devices usually hold a grimoire or scepter form housing deep routine libraries, and their work is healing, enhancement, reactor replenishment, barrier anchoring, and the disruption or hijacking of enemy devices. A capable Thaumaturge keeps a wounded team in the fight, lifts allies past their usual limits for a bounded window, and can turn a hostile device against its owner through an intrusion routine. In sustained operations they are frequently the most valuable mage on the field—which also makes them the first target.

A Barrier Jacket is armor that exists mostly where attacks cannot reach it. It forms when a mage circulates Mana through their device under a pre-loaded defensive routine, raising a layered higher-dimensional field anchored to the body. Because most of the structure sits outside baseline space, a Jacket adds effectively no weight and imposes no meaningful restriction on movement while still intercepting both kinetic and mana-based attacks—the single most important reason trained mages survive engagements that would shred conventional body armor.

Within the field's constraints, appearance is almost entirely the operator's choice, and a Jacket became the clearest statement a mage makes about themselves. Some run formal silhouettes—ballroom, royal, aristocratic, ceremonial—that carry protection inside something elegant; others favor military dress, hard tactical rigs, or scholar's and street cuts. The style is cosmetic, but it is rarely arbitrary: affiliation, role, and temperament all read off a mage's Jacket at a glance.

Underneath the styling, a Jacket is a working defensive system and a secondary Mana store. Its layered barriers absorb a bounded amount of damage before a layer fails, and the device re-knits failed layers for as long as the operator sustains the routine and has the reactor capacity to feed it. More advanced Jackets fold in utility—sealed environmental protection, integrated comms and sensors, and limited magical-thrust flight—turning a survival tool into a mobile platform, with every added function drawing against the same finite Mana budget.

Two pressures made a dedicated agency unavoidable. The first was sheer numbers: beyond the supervised activations the program intended, mages began appearing on their own—rare accidental core activations, and, increasingly, children born with reactors already active, an inherited awakening the first generation of mages passed to the second. The second was the rogue corporations, pushing untracked devices and unlicensed awakenings into circulation faster than any existing agency could follow. By the early 2020s the registered mage population had climbed well into the hundreds of thousands and showed no sign of leveling.

Congress answered by chartering the Mages Association Bureau, a federal agency whose jurisdiction over mana-tech supersedes state and local authority and which operates, under classified directives, with latitude conventional law enforcement does not have. Registration is mandatory: every activated mage must file rank, capabilities, and bonded device with the Bureau. Concealing an active reactor or an unregistered device is a federal felony, and the Bureau audits the licensed corporations that build the hardware as rigorously as it tracks the people who carry it.

Above all, the Bureau keeps the secret. To the public there is no Mana, no reactor, no awakening—the field is classified at a tier most of the government will never be read into. Every mage signs a binding non-disclosure instrument as a condition of activation, and the Bureau issues each a prepared cover: to family, employers, and neighbors, a newly awakened mage has simply been recruited into a selective federal research initiative or military program, an account that explains the clearances, the absences, and the stipend without ever touching the truth. Disclosure to an uncleared civilian is prosecuted as harshly as any device crime—secrecy is not a courtesy the Bureau extends, but the first duty it enforces.

Control runs through the devices as much as the people. Every legally manufactured Intelligent Device carries Bureau tracking and a remote-shutdown pathway the agency can trigger if its registered operator is confirmed in illegal use—the same pathway whose absence makes rogue-fabricated, untraceable devices such a priority to find. The Bureau maintains linked registries of mages, devices, and licensed routines, and it is the hunt for what those registries cannot see that drives most of its field work.

Access scales with role, and the top of the scale is held back. Civilians generally carry first- or second-generation devices; professional and corporate mages run third; Bureau agents and military units field fourth. Fifth-generation hardware stays under tight restriction, released only to vetted operators and sanctioned research. The Bureau is more than a cordon, though—it funds research, trains the newly awakened, employs mages in public service, and investigates mana crime with expertise no ordinary department can match, keeping cooperation more rewarding than evasion. You will register here. Whatever you become, this is where your file begins.

For all its spread inside American borders, mana-tech is a national monopoly, and that is no accident of geography. It remains effectively unknown abroad because the Arcaea Division keeps it that way. Arcaea is the country's elite mage corps, and its Suppression Teams operate worldwide with one standing mission: find any foreign effort that stumbles onto Mana and bury it—data, instruments, and institutional memory—before it can take root. They work by erasure rather than open confrontation, and their success is measured in discoveries that never become public anywhere else on Earth.

Arcaea does not stand alone. Behind it is the Aerospace Fleet, a line of space- and FTL-capable warships running on pure mana-tech. The classes descend from the Leviathan-class Aerospace Battleships—kilometers-long capital ships—through cruisers down to the nimble Aerospace Destroyers. Every hull is driven by an Artificial Mana Reactor, an engineered analogue of the human organ built to power a ship rather than a person, and armed with Array Cannons for ranged engagement and Anti-Matter Torpedoes for strategic strikes. Nothing in any conventional navy on the planet operates on the same axis.

The Fleet's day-to-day work is quieter than its armament suggests. It patrols the solar system, flies long-duration research missions, and has spent years raising secret colonies across the planets—habitats and shipyards sited well outside the reach of civilian astronomy, extending the American mana order off-world the same way the Bureau extends it at home. The exclusivity Arcaea enforces on the ground holds in space as well: as far as the Fleet is concerned, there is no competition up there yet.

The program's farthest reach to date belongs to the exploration ship Voyager 4, the first vessel to pass the Oort Cloud and cross into a neighboring star system under FTL transit. It returned an enormous dataset—stellar, planetary, and navigational—routed in sanitized form to NASA, where it quietly reshaped a generation of research whose authors will never be cleared to know its source. Somewhere past the edge of the solar system, the quietest consequence of a bankrupt corporation's discovery is still unfolding.
